# AI UGC Ad Prompts: 15 Fill-in Templates for Creator-Style Video Ads

- [Ideoy](https://ideoy.ai) - Remake this week's meme videos with one selfie (AI image-to-video, freemium, adults only).
AI UGC ad generation is a production workflow rather than one model: a creator-style scene may combine generated video, product references, a speaking avatar or voice, captions, and ordinary editing. Claims, permissions, disclosures, and product fidelity still require human review.

> Useful AI UGC ad templates specify a believable creator, filming context, product interaction, spoken hook, proof mechanism, and next step. Replace every bracket with facts you are authorized to advertise; these are fill-in templates, not finished claims.

Created by [Reviral](https://reviral.ai), an AI video studio for model-aware video and creator workflows.

<a id="how-to-use"></a>
## Before you fill in a template

- Replace brackets with verified facts; never invent results, reviews, scarcity, certifications, or endorsements.
- Obtain permission for people, voices, likenesses, logos, music, and customer content.
- Match disclosures to the platform and jurisdiction.
- Composite approved packaging, UI, captions, and exact text in post when generation is unreliable.

<a id="choose-a-workflow"></a>
## Choose a workflow

| Workflow | Best for | Prompting note |
|---|---|---|
| Fill-in scene template | Best for drafting a creator concept and spoken hook. | Replace every bracket with an approved fact before rendering. |
| Image-to-video product demo | Best when exact product appearance matters. | Use approved reference assets and keep hand actions simple. |
| Talking creator or avatar | Best for direct-to-camera delivery. | Use an authorized likeness and voice; reserve room for disclosures. |
| Edit and composite | Required for exact packaging, UI, captions, and source clips. | Replace unreliable generated details with approved assets in post. |

Version-specific features change. Confirm the selected model and interface controls before rendering.

## Discovery & Demo

### 1. Bathroom-shelf discovery

```text
Vertical creator-style ad filmed on a phone in a bright apartment bathroom. A relatable creator in their late twenties holds [PRODUCT] beside the sink within the first second and says, "I bought this because [HONEST PROBLEM], but the part I didn't expect was [VERIFIED DIFFERENTIATOR]." They demonstrate one simple use step, cut to a close product texture shot, then return to selfie framing: "If you also [AUDIENCE NEED], here's what to check out." Natural window light, slight handheld movement, conversational delivery, captions-safe center framing, approved package shown accurately.
```

**Technique note:** Product and problem appear immediately. “Verified differentiator” prevents the prompt from manufacturing proof.

### 3. Problem-solution desk demo

```text
Vertical home-office UGC ad. Open on the frustrating moment: [COMMON PROBLEM] happening at a cluttered desk. The creator enters frame, says, "This was the tiny thing wrecking my workflow," and places [PRODUCT] beside the problem for scale. Overhead insert shows one uncomplicated setup step and the product solving [SPECIFIC VERIFIED USE CASE]. Return to medium selfie: "That's it—no dramatic life makeover, just [PRACTICAL BENEFIT]." Daylight, real desk sounds, brisk edits, legible composition, accurate product appearance.
```

**Technique note:** Showing the before state makes the solution causal. The anti-hype line keeps the payoff proportionate.

### 12. Hands-only how-to

```text
Vertical hands-only UGC tutorial on a clean countertop. Place [PRODUCT] and only the required accessories in frame. Voiceover: "Here's the setup I wish I'd seen first." Demonstrate exactly three manufacturer-approved steps: [STEP 1], [STEP 2], [STEP 3]. After each step, pause long enough for an editor to add a label. Show the valid end state and say, "Before using it, also check [REAL SAFETY OR COMPATIBILITY NOTE]." Locked overhead camera, bright soft light, normal speed, no invented controls or functions.
```

**Technique note:** Hands-only staging is efficient, but exact instructions must come from approved documentation. Pauses make post-production text safer.

<a id="diary-and-testimonial"></a>
## Diary & Testimonial

### 2. Three-day diary

```text
Vertical UGC diary assembled from three plainly labeled moments: DAY 1, DAY 2, DAY 3. The same creator, hairstyle, gray T-shirt, and kitchen background remain consistent. On day one they introduce [PRODUCT] and the realistic goal [GOAL]. On day two they show how it fits into their routine. On day three they report only [OBSERVED, SUPPORTABLE EXPERIENCE] and say, "Your experience may be different, but this is what I noticed." Casual phone exposure, jump cuts, honest unscripted pauses, no beauty filter.
```

**Technique note:** Consistency anchors reduce diary drift. The result must be a real, supportable observation—not a fabricated testimonial.

### 15. Quiet lifestyle testimonial

```text
Vertical low-key testimonial in a lived-in living room at evening. The creator folds laundry while [PRODUCT] sits naturally within reach. They say, "I don't think this is for everyone, but it helped me with [GENUINE PERSONAL USE] because [SUPPORTABLE REASON]." Cut to one close demonstration, then return to the same composition: "What I'd want you to know first is [REAL CAVEAT]." Warm lamp light, gentle handheld camera, ordinary pauses and breaths, no dramatic before-and-after, no medical or guaranteed outcome language.
```

**Technique note:** Activity prevents a static talking head, while the caveat keeps the testimony bounded. Use only a real authorized experience.

<a id="comparisons-and-objections"></a>
## Comparisons & Objections

### 4. Grocery-bag comparison

```text
Vertical kitchen-counter comparison ad filmed by a friend. The creator removes [PRODUCT] and [ALTERNATIVE CATEGORY, NOT A TRADEMARK] from a grocery bag and says, "I use each for something different." They demonstrate one fair criterion at a time: [CRITERION 1], [CRITERION 2], [CRITERION 3]. End with, "Choose [PRODUCT] if you care most about [VERIFIED STRENGTH]; choose the other type if [HONEST TRADEOFF]." Neutral tone, single continuous countertop geography, simple hand movements, natural room audio, on-screen labels added accurately in post.
```

**Technique note:** A conditional recommendation is more credible than a universal winner claim. Compare equivalent facts and avoid disparagement.

### 8. Car confession hook

```text
Vertical parked-car creator ad recorded safely with the vehicle stationary. Tight selfie framing. The creator holds [PRODUCT] and opens: "I almost returned this for one reason." After a short pause they explain [REAL INITIAL OBJECTION], then show the feature or instruction that resolved it. Insert a close product shot filmed outside the car if needed. End: "It won't be for you if [HONEST DISQUALIFIER], but it worked for my [SPECIFIC USE]." Natural daylight, light road ambience, candid pacing, no driving footage.
```

**Technique note:** The reversal earns attention without fake outrage. An honest disqualifier qualifies the audience and keeps the pitch credible.

<a id="unboxing-and-gifting"></a>
## Unboxing & Gifting

### 5. Unboxing without the hype

```text
Vertical overhead-plus-selfie unboxing of [PRODUCT] on a wooden dining table. The creator says, "Here's exactly what arrived—no cinematic unboxing." Show the sealed package, open it with one clean cut, lay out each included item in order, and name only the contents listed by the manufacturer. Cut to selfie: "My first impression: [SUBJECTIVE, NON-PERFORMANCE OBSERVATION]. I'll test it before saying more." Soft daylight, normal phone autofocus, packaging and labels preserved, no invented accessories.
```

**Technique note:** Inventory is verifiable and useful. Separating first impression from tested performance avoids an unsupported review.

### 10. Gift-recipient POV

```text
Vertical first-person gift scenario. Camera represents the recipient seated at a kitchen table. A friend slides a wrapped [PRODUCT] into frame and says, "You mentioned [REAL NEED], so I found this." Recipient hands unwrap it; cut to the friend explaining one verified reason for the choice. End on the product placed in its normal use context and the line, "Thoughtful because [PRACTICAL FIT], not because it costs the most." Warm evening light, natural dialogue overlap, simple two-person staging, accurate packaging.
```

**Technique note:** The use need motivates the product. First-person framing avoids generating an unnecessary additional face.

<a id="social-formats"></a>
## Social Formats

### 6. Comment-reply ad

```text
Vertical comment-reply UGC ad. Begin with space at the top for an approved comment added in post: "[REAL CUSTOMER QUESTION]." The creator points once toward that space and says, "Short answer: [ACCURATE ANSWER]." They hold [PRODUCT] at chest height and demonstrate the relevant feature in two simple steps. Finish: "The detail that matters is [LIMITATION OR CONDITION], so check [OFFICIAL SOURCE] before deciding." Clean bedroom background, friendly direct eye contact, restrained jump cuts, product visible within two seconds.
```

**Technique note:** The limitation builds trust and reduces overclaiming. Use a real or clearly dramatized question, never a fake customer identity.

### 14. Stitch-style myth check

```text
Vertical stitch-style ad with blank left-side space for a licensed source clip added later. The creator listens, then says, "That claim needs context." They hold [PRODUCT] and separate one myth from one documented fact: "[MYTH OR OVERSTATEMENT]" versus "[VERIFIED FACT WITH CONDITION]." Demonstrate only what can be visibly shown, then direct viewers to [PRIMARY SOURCE OR PRODUCT PAGE]. Neutral educational tone, respectful delivery, simple punch-in edit, clear synthetic-media and sponsorship disclosure where applicable.
```

**Technique note:** The source clip must be licensed and accurately represented. “Needs context” invites correction without manufacturing conflict.

<a id="founder-and-faq"></a>
## Founder & FAQ

### 7. Founder packing an order

```text
Vertical small-business founder ad in a real packing area. The founder places [PRODUCT] in a shipping box, adds [ACTUAL INCLUDED MATERIAL], and says, "We made this for people who [TRUE CUSTOMER NEED]." While sealing the box, they explain one specific design choice and why it exists, using only documented product facts. End by writing a thank-you note and saying, "If that sounds like you, you can learn more at [DESTINATION]." Warm practical light, tactile packing sounds, modest handheld camera, sincere low-key delivery.
```

**Technique note:** A real process supplies visual proof. Founder status and the design rationale must be authentic.

### 11. FAQ rapid three

```text
Vertical UGC FAQ with one creator against a plain warm wall, holding [PRODUCT]. Hook: "Three things I wanted to know before ordering." Use three clean jump cuts. Question one: [REAL FAQ]; answer with [VERIFIED ANSWER]. Question two: [REAL FAQ]; answer with [VERIFIED ANSWER]. Question three: [REAL FAQ]; answer with [VERIFIED ANSWER OR CLEAR UNKNOWN]. End: "Check the current details at [OFFICIAL DESTINATION]." Centered chest-up framing, energetic natural delivery, room for accurate captions, no floating generated text.
```

**Technique note:** Three questions create pace without a montage of locations. Add exact captions during editing rather than trusting generated typography.

<a id="lifestyle-integrations"></a>
## Lifestyle Integrations

### 9. Get-ready-with-me integration

```text
Vertical get-ready-with-me ad in a bedroom mirror setup. The creator talks about [RELATABLE OCCASION] while naturally using [PRODUCT] at the correct point in the routine. Hook: "I have ten minutes, so I'm only doing the steps that matter." Show one straightforward application or setup action, then a final look under the same lighting. Voice line: "The reason this stays in my routine is [SUPPORTABLE BENEFIT]." Casual jump cuts, consistent face and outfit, approved disclosure visible near the opening, no exaggerated transformation.
```

**Technique note:** The product is embedded in a familiar format, not bolted on at the end. Same-light comparison prevents a deceptive visual result.

### 13. Pack-with-me travel use

```text
Vertical pack-with-me ad beside an open carry-on. The creator says, "This earns space in my bag because [VERIFIED TRAVEL BENEFIT]." They place [PRODUCT] next to a common object for honest scale, show how it packs using one simple movement, and mention [REAL LIMITATION: SIZE, POWER, LIQUID, REGION, OR COMPATIBILITY]. Finish with the bag closing naturally and: "Check your carrier and destination rules before travel." Bright bedroom daylight, casual phone camera, realistic product dimensions, no airport-security claims.
```

**Technique note:** Scale and limitation are more useful than vague portability. Travel rules vary, so the prompt explicitly avoids promising compliance.

## FAQ

**Why are these called fill-in templates?**

Every scene contains placeholders that must be replaced with approved product facts, proof, caveats, and destinations before use.

**Can I invent a testimonial for a concept ad?**

No. Use a real authorized experience or label a dramatization clearly and have claims reviewed for the intended market.

**What should always be added in post?**

Use post-production for exact captions, disclosures, packaging, UI, trademarks, licensed source clips, and any detail that must be correct.

## Formula recap

Start with the formula near the top, keep one dominant action chain, and change one variable per iteration. Generated results remain probabilistic and must be reviewed before use.
